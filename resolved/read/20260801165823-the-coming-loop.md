  display_title: Harness loops, legibility, and responsibility
  summary: Ronacher distinguishes the coding agent's internal loop from the harness loop that keeps work alive after the model says it is done. He argues that loops already work well for bounded, mechanically verifiable, or short-lived outputs such as ports, performance experiments, research, and security scanning. For lasting code, however, hands-off loops amplify local defenses, duplication, weak invariants, and dependence on machines to understand machine-authored systems. The durable problem is not whether teams will use loops, but how architecture and tooling preserve legibility, judgment, and accountable human supervision.
